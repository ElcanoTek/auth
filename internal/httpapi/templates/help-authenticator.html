Set up two-factor sign-in

Three steps, about five minutes.

What it is

An extra six-digit code, from an app on your phone, that you enter after your password to keep your account secure.

Why it matters

  • Passwords get stolen and reused.
  • AI makes phishing emails and fake sign-in pages hard to spot.
  • With two-factor on, a stolen password alone is not enough.

1. Open an authenticator app

Install one if you need to, then open it: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or Bitwarden Authenticator (all free), or 1Password.

Scan from inside the app. Your phone's regular camera app cannot add the account to your authenticator.

2. Add your account

  • Set-up page on a computer: in the app, tap +, choose Scan a QR code and point your phone at the QR code (1).
  • Set-up page on this phone: copy the setup key (2). In the app, tap +, choose Enter a setup key, paste it and pick Time based.

Exact buttons for your app:

Google Authenticator
  • Tap +, then Scan a QR code.
  • Or tap +, then Enter a setup key: account {{.Issuer}}, paste the key, Time based, Add.

Google's guide (opens in a new tab)

Microsoft Authenticator
  • Tap +, then Other account (Google, Facebook, etc.), not "Work or school account", and scan.
  • Or, on the camera screen, tap Enter code manually: account {{.Issuer}}, paste the key as the secret key, Finish.

Microsoft's guide (opens in a new tab)

Bitwarden
  • Bitwarden Authenticator: tap + and scan, or tap Enter key manually and paste the key. Choose Save here.
  • Bitwarden Password Manager (Premium or a paid plan): edit the login, Set up TOTP, scan or paste the key, Save.

Authenticator guide (opens in a new tab) · Password Manager guide (opens in a new tab)

1Password
  • Edit the login for {{.Brand}}, select Add More, then One-Time Password.
  • Scan the QR code (on a computer, 1Password reads it from your screen) or paste the key, then Save.

1Password's guide (opens in a new tab)

3. Enter the code

Type the six-digit code the app shows into the code box (3) and select Turn on two-factor sign-in.

Opened this guide from the set-up page? Switch back to that tab.

Save your recovery codes

Next you will see ten recovery codes, shown only once. Each one lets you sign in a single time if you lose or replace your phone, so keep them somewhere safe, such as your password manager.

Trouble?

Scanned with your phone's camera
Open your authenticator app and scan from there instead.
"Please match the requested format" or "That looks like the setup key"
The key goes into your app. The code box takes the six digits the app shows.
"That code did not match" or "did not work"
Set your phone's date and time to automatic, then try the current code.
"That set-up code expired"
Add the new QR code, and once it works, delete the older entries.
Your app lists {{.Issuer}} twice
Only one entry works. Try the other, then delete the one that fails.
New or lost phone
Move your codes with your app's transfer or export option before resetting a phone. Lost it? Sign in with a recovery code, or ask your administrator to reset two-factor sign-in.
